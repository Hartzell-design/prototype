Case Study · NULLDRIVE · Slipstream DS

One design system, five releases:
how a racing HUD evolves without rewrites.

NULLDRIVE launched with a fast, readable HUD. Over the next two years it added accessibility, live seasons, an AI race engineer, and a mobile port. This is how we built the foundation that made each step a token change, not a redesign.

01

The Problem

A racing HUD has about 200 ms to tell a player what they need before their eyes return to the road. At launch, that was the whole job.

The roadmap didn't stop at launch. Every new feature, like seasons, coaching, and touch controls, wanted a piece of that same 200 ms. Without a system, every addition becomes a bespoke overlay, the HUD gets noisy, and each platform drifts into its own fork.

So the brief wasn't to "design a HUD." It was to design the thing that designs the HUD.

READABLE AT SPEED

Glanceable numbers, fixed slot positions, and color used only when it carries meaning.

ROOM TO GROW

Reserved zones and semantic tokens for features that didn't exist yet.

ONE SYSTEM, EVERY SCREEN

The same components on TV, monitor, and phone, with density handled by tokens.

Built to Scale with AI

01 · TOKENS AS API

Change meaning, not pixels

Components read semantic tokens, so a theme, a season, or a platform is a new token set instead of a fork.

02 · RESERVED FOR AI

AI gets its own lane

--ai violet and slot.ai were reserved at v1.0, so when AI shipped, players could always tell machine guidance apart from game state.

03 · HUMAN IN THE LOOP

Guardrails by design

Only one callout at a time, a confidence threshold, a mute that persists, and adaptive difficulty that's always disclosed and never hidden.

04 · LIVE PROTOTYPES

Prototype in the real medium

Every decision was tested in running HTML prototypes like this one, not static frames. That's why stakeholders could feel the 200 ms.
